# United States — RN pathway (overview)

**Audience:** internationally educated nurses exploring US licensure and work.
**Not legal advice.** Always verify with the state board and USCIS.

## License boards & NCLEX
- Nursing licensure in the US is **state-based**. Start with the board of nursing in the state where you want to practice.
- Most jurisdictions use the **NCLEX-RN** exam developed by NCSBN.
- NCSBN overview of NCLEX: https://www.ncsbn.org/nclex.htm
- NCSBN main site: https://www.ncsbn.org/
- Find a board of nursing directory via NCSBN: https://www.ncsbn.org/nursing-regulation/nursing-jurisdiction.page

## Example state board (New York)
- New York State Education Department (NYSED) Office of the Professions — Nursing: https://www.op.nysed.gov/professions/nursing
- NYSED lists license requirements, applications, and fees for RNs. Confirm current forms on that page before applying.
- **Uncertain:** exact fee amounts and processing times change — check NYSED, do not rely on third-party blogs.

## Credentials evaluation
- Many boards require a credentials evaluation for internationally educated nurses (e.g. CGFNS or board-approved evaluators). Which evaluator is accepted **depends on the state**.
- CGFNS International (commonly referenced, not universal): https://www.cgfns.org/
- **Uncertain:** whether a given state accepts CGFNS vs another evaluator — confirm with that state's board.

## Work authorization (high level)
- Permanent employment-based third preference (EB-3) overview from USCIS: https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/permanent-workers/employment-based-immigration-third-preference-eb-3
- Temporary nonimmigrant options (e.g. H-1B) have separate rules and nursing roles often do not fit every category — verify with USCIS and counsel.
- USCIS working in the United States hub: https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states
- **Uncertain / do not invent:** visa wait times, priority dates, salary ranges, pass rates.

## Study notes
- Some nurses pursue US-based bridging or graduate programs; school accreditation and state board acceptance must be checked case by case.
- US Department of Education accreditation database (for schools): https://www.ed.gov/accreditation

## Quick checklist
1. Pick target state → open that board of nursing site.
2. Confirm internationally educated nurse (IEN) requirements + NCLEX eligibility.
3. Plan credentials evaluation + English proficiency if required by that board.
4. Treat immigration path (EB-3 employer sponsorship, etc.) as a **separate** track from licensure.
